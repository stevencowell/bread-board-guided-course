# Preparing to apply an oil finish

Before any finish is prepared, the breadboard surface must be checked carefully. The timber should feel smooth and consistent, with no obvious scratches, dents, glue residue, dust-filled corners or unexplained marks. Inspect the broad faces, edges and end grain under good light, then run a clean hand lightly over the surface to identify rough areas that may be difficult to see. If a defect cannot be explained or safely corrected, stop and ask the teacher before continuing.

The workspace must be clear, clean and ready for the task. Remove tools, offcuts, sanding dust and unrelated materials from the bench. A cluttered area increases the chance of knocking the project, spilling material or transferring dirt onto the timber. Follow all teacher directions about where finishing preparation will occur and what protective equipment or workspace controls are required.

> **Key idea:** A finish can only protect and enhance a surface that has been properly checked, cleaned and handled.

Handle the breadboard carefully during preparation. Clean hands are essential because grease, sweat and dirt can contaminate the surface. Lift the project by suitable areas rather than dragging it across the bench. Avoid placing it on dusty, rough or stained surfaces. Once the final surface check has been completed, unnecessary handling should be kept to a minimum.

Use only the materials supplied or approved by the teacher. Do not substitute containers, cloths, cleaning materials or finishing products from another task. Keep approved materials closed or protected until they are required. Dust, water, food, metal filings and other workshop contaminants must be kept away from the project and finishing area.

Rags and waste materials must be managed exactly as directed by the teacher. Used or contaminated materials must not be left on benches, placed in pockets or mixed with general workshop waste. Put them immediately in the designated location or container. This helps keep the workspace orderly and reduces unnecessary hazards.

During preparation, protect the breadboard from scratches, fingerprints, dust and accidental contact. Place it only on the approved clean support and keep other students’ work away from the area. Careful preparation reduces rework and helps ensure the surface remains ready for the next stage.

## Finish-preparation check

- Surface inspected and free from visible defects.
- Workspace clear, clean and teacher-approved.
- Hands and approved materials clean.
- Project handled and supported carefully.
- Contamination kept away from the timber.
- Rags and waste managed as directed.
- Breadboard protected until finishing begins.
